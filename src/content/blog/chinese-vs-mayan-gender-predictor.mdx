Two ancient-looking methods dominate the gender-guessing corner of the internet: the **Chinese gender chart** and the **Mayan gender predictor**. They look similar — calendars, ages, mysterious origins — but they work on completely different logic.

## The Chinese method: lunar chart lookup

- **Inputs:** mother's birth date + conception date
- **Logic:** convert both to the Chinese lunisolar calendar; look up a fixed 28×12 grid cell
- **Claimed history:** Qing dynasty palace chart, ~700 years old
- **Accuracy when tested:** ~50% — coin flip

## The Mayan method: odd/even parity

- **Inputs:** mother's age at conception + the conception *year*
- **Logic:** odd + odd or even + even → girl; mixed → boy
- **Claimed history:** Mayan civilization, but no confirmed manuscript survives
- **Accuracy when tested:** 50% by construction — parity is weirdly deterministic folklore

## If they disagree, who wins?

Medicine does. **NIPT (~10 weeks)** and the **anatomy scan (18–20 weeks)** are the only reliable confirmations. If both folklore methods agreed... also medicine. If they disagreed and you polled the ring test and the baking-soda test too — see the pattern? Any of these lands at or near 50%.
