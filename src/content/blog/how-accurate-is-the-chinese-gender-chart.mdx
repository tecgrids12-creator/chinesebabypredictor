Somewhere out there is a claim that the Chinese gender chart is **93% accurate**. It's everywhere — and it's never had a source. Let's look at what happens when people actually test it.

## What studies actually measured

The best-controlled look came from the University of Michigan Health Lab, which analyzed the chart's predictions against real births and found the result was about **50%** — precisely what you'd expect from random guessing, given only two possible outcomes. Official summaries of the research conclude plainly: don't paint the nursery just yet.

Claimed accuracy rates (70%, 90%, 93%) trace back to marketing, not to peer-reviewed data — they circulate because they travel well.

## Why it *feels* so accurate

- **A 50/50 base rate** means the chart is "right" half the time for every single user who tries it.
- **Confirmation bias** makes a correct prediction memorable; a miss quietly disappears.
- **Social reinforcement** repeats the accurate anecdotes at baby showers while the misses never make it into family lore.

That's not a flaw in your family — it's how human pattern-finding works. It's the same psychology behind sports fans' "lucky shirts."
