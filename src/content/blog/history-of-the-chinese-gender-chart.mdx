## The legend

The tale goes: ~700 years ago, a chart predicting babies' sex was buried with a royal near Beijing. The Qing imperial family (1644–1911) supposedly kept it secret in the **palace (hence 清宫图 — the "Qing Palace Chart")**, using it to plan for male heirs. When the dynasty collapsed, story says the chart was looted during the Boxer Rebellion, translated in England, resurfaced in Austria, and finally published in a Taiwanese newspaper by a scholar in 1972.

## What historians can actually verify

- No primary manuscript dating to the Qing or earlier has been demonstrated for the chart as circulated today.
- The chart's *style* draws on genuinely old traditions — the **I Ching**, **yin & yang**, and the **Five Elements (五行)**: metal, water, wood, fire, earth.
- The "Beijing Institute of Science" attribution that accompanies many chart versions online has no documented primary source.
- Regardless of provenance, the chart undeniably spread globally in the late 20th century and became a beloved pregnancy tradition.

Like Chinese New Year red envelopes or the ring test, the chart's value is **ritual and connection** — a shared moment with family at a high-emotion time.
