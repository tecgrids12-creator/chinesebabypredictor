Lunar years with **13 months** occur roughly seven times every 19 years, and the lunar year that began January 29, 2025 is one of them: it carries a **leap (intercalary) 6th month**, extending it to about 384 days and pushing Chinese New Year 2026 way out to February 17.

## Why this matters for 2025 conceptions

If you conceived between **roughly July 25 and August 22, 2025**, your conception date fell in that leap 6th month. The traditional chart has no leap column, so:

- **Our approach:** map leap-month conceptions to the base month (month 6), show a note on the result, and if your date is near a leap boundary, both candidate months are shown.
- Many competitor predictors simply pretend the leap month doesn't exist and produce off-by-one results all month.

## How to check yours

1. Run the [lunar age calculator](/lunar-age-calculator/) — it prints your full lunar date including the leap flag.
2. Suspicious of a boundary? Try both the leap candidate and its base month on the [interactive chart](/chinese-gender-chart/) — if they agree, boundary anxiety resolved; if not, use ours' documented convention.

## The takeaway

Leap months expose how "one chart, one answer" claims collapse at calendar seams — which is why a predictor that *shows its conversion* is infinitely more trustworthy than a photocopied grid image with a mystique caption. We prefer integrity over mystery. 🌓
