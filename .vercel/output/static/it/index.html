What Is the Chinese Baby Predictor?

The Chinese Baby Predictor is a free online tool that estimates whether a baby is likely to be a boy or a girl using the ancient Chinese gender chart — a 700-year-old lunar table credited to Qing Dynasty imperial scholars. Enter the mother's date of birth and the conception date: the calculator converts both to the Chinese lunar calendar and instantly reads the traditional prediction. It is a cultural tradition for entertainment, not a medical test — for a certain answer, ask your licensed healthcare provider.

Gregorian vs. Chinese Lunar Age Calculation

Gregorian (solar) age

The age you know from your passport: counted from your birthday on the Gregorian calendar, adding one year every January 1st... or rather, every birthday.

Chinese lunar age (虚岁)

Traditional Chinese reckoning: you are one year old at birth and gain a year at every Lunar New Year — plus conception adds nine months in the chart’s counting. That is why the predictor shows your lunar age one or two years older. Convert your dates instantly →

The chart’s roots reach back to the Qing Dynasty imperial records and the philosophy of the I Ching (Book of Changes), while the best-known version of the table is said to have been recovered from the Forbidden City in Beijing. Whatever its origin story, the grid couples the mother’s lunar age with the lunar month of conception — the exact conversion our calculator performs for you.

How Leap Months (Runyue 闰月) Affect Your Prediction

Why other calculators disagree

The Chinese lunisolar calendar inserts an intercalary (leap) month about seven times every nineteen years, so a year has 13 months. Many simpler baby-gender calculators treat that extra month as a bare number — others ignore it. When the same conception date is mapped to a different month number, the same couple gets two different predictions. Our converter does not guess: it maps the leap month against the official 1900–2100 lunisolar tables and shows the leap flag in your result.

The traditional half-month rule

Classic practice assigns a conception that falls in a leap month by halves:

  1. First half of the leap month (day 1 to ~15) → counted as month N, the month it repeats.
  2. Second half (day ~16 onward) → counted as month N+1, the month that follows.

Example: a conception in leap 6月’s second half is read as month 7 on the chart. Not every modern calculator follows this convention — that is the “conflicting results” you see.

Jump straight to the interactive chart or lunar age calculator to see both halves of any leap month mapped for you.

The Swedish Registry Scientific Study Breakdown

The largest empirical test of the lunar gender chart is a population-based study by Eduardo Villamor and colleagues analysing Swedish national birth-register data covering roughly 2.8 million births. Each mother’s lunar age and the lunar month of conception were reproduced exactly as the traditional chart prescribes, then compared against the baby’s actual sex.

The result was statistically indistinguishable from a fair coin — about 50.0% accuracy, with no lunar month or age row performing meaningfully above chance. That is not a design flaw: male sperm carry an X, half carry a Y chromosome, so biology fixes the chances of each conception at roughly even odds before any calendar is consulted. No lookup table can move that probability — which is why charts that promise 93% have no mechanism to back it up. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How accurate is the Chinese gender predictor?

+

Honestly? About the same as a coin flip — roughly 50%. Claims of 70–93% accuracy circulate online, but controlled studies have found no evidence the chart can predict a baby’s sex. We present it as a beloved tradition, not a medical test, and our tool shows exactly how the lunar dates are converted so you can follow along.

What is the Chinese gender calendar and where does it come from?

+

It’s a traditional chart said to date back 700 years and to have been discovered in a royal tomb near Beijing, with other versions tracing it to the Qing dynasty (1644–1911). Historians can’t verify these tales, but the chart has circled the globe as a fun pregnancy ritual. It predicts using the mother’s lunar age and lunar month of conception.

Do I need to know my lunar age or lunar dates?

+

No. Our predictor converts your birth date and conception date into exact lunar dates automatically — using the full Chinese lunisolar calendar tables (1900–2100), so dates near Chinese New Year or leap months are handled correctly.

I’m not sure of my conception date. Can I still use it?

+

Yes. Switch the second input to “Baby’s due date” (we estimate conception ≈ due date − 266 days) or “Last period started” (conception ≈ LMP + 14 days). Small date shifts can matter near lunar month boundaries, so exact dates will always be most precise.

Does the Chinese gender chart change every year?

+

No — there’s one authentic chart with the same boy/girl cells every year. What changes is the calendar conversion: Chinese New Year moves (Feb 17, 2026, for example) and lunar months start on different Gregorian dates, so the same Western dates can land in different cells each year.

Why is my lunar age one or two years older than my real age?

+

Chinese tradition counts you as age 1 at birth (pregnancy time counts), and you gain a year at Chinese New Year rather than on your birthday. So your lunar age is usually your Western age plus one, sometimes plus two — our lunar age calculator works it out instantly.

Can the chart or planner really choose my baby’s sex?

+

No. A baby’s sex is determined by chromosomes at conception, and no calendar can influence it. Use the planner for fun and for understanding how the chart is read — then leave family planning decisions to you and your healthcare provider.

How can I know my baby’s sex for real?

+

Medical methods only: the NIPT blood test can indicate fetal sex from about 10 weeks, and the 18–20-week anatomy ultrasound is the classic confirmation moment. Both are dramatically more reliable than any folklore method.
